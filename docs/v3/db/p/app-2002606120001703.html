Direction

Open a focused weekly meal-planning gap by letting users specify budget, preferred supermarket, dietary preferences, calorie goals, and ingredients already available, then generate a personalized plan.

The product addresses a recurring planning decision with multiple practical constraints, while recent nutrition and food-tracking discussions indicate an active problem space.

On the German App Store listing captured for 2026-11-19, mise described personalized meal plans based on nutritional preferences, calorie goals, preferred supermarkets, budget, and ingredients already at home; the release date was 2026-06-10.

Result By 2026-11-19, the app was ranked #3 in DE Top Grossing · Food & Drink and #158 in DE Top Grossing overall; the product had 2,111 ratings at 4.51/5 and 25 ratings per day. These figures are not download or revenue counts, and no evidence identifies the chart movement as caused by this positioning. Cause unproven

Growth

Pair a completed utility action with a social exchange: let users share a favorite dish and receive budget-planning advice from peers.

A community layer can turn a private utility into visible peer activity and create reasons to return, though the available evidence does not isolate this effect.

On 2026-11-11, mise shipped v1.1.40 and wrote: “Unsere neue Community-Funktion ist da! Vernetze dich mit anderen, teile deine Lieblingsgerichte und tausche Tipps zum Thema Budgetplanung aus.” The release also said: “Wenn dir unsere App gefällt, freuen wir uns sehr über eine Bewertung.”

Result On 2026-11-18, mise was first seen on a tracked chart; by the capture it had risen 136 places to #6 in ID Top Grossing · Food & Drink, with #3 in DE and #7 in US equivalents. The chart rank is not a new-user count, and the evidence does not show that community activation caused the lift. Cause unproven

Money

Launch the planner free, reserve premium planning or shopping capabilities for in-app purchase, and place the paywall after the first useful weekly plan.

Free utility acquisition can build trust before monetization, but the evidence does not establish which feature is locked or whether users pay.

If it were you

DoDo not clone the app wholesale; test whether a sharper budget-first meal-planning workflow plus a small community is viable. The evidence leaves localization, pricing, conversion, and the actual acquisition channel open, while the rating request and two-day history limit attribution confidence.

What it is

mise is a free meal-planning app that creates personalized plans from dietary preferences, calorie goals, preferred supermarkets, budget, and ingredients already at home. It targets weekly preparation, healthier eating, grocery savings, and reduced food waste.

Where users come from

The evidence does not identify an acquisition post, ad, launch, roundup, or referral source. A community update shipped on 2026-11-11, and the app was first seen on a tracked chart on 2026-11-18, but the source of its users remains unknown.

How it makes money

The app is listed as free with in-app purchases and appears in top-grossing charts. The available evidence does not state the paid plan, price, trial, locked features, or exact point of purchase.

Who built it

The developer is TEGS LABS LTD. No founder background, team size, funding, or pre-existing audience is included in the evidence.

What it does badly

The product's localization and onboarding quality are not documented, and the evidence gives no install, retention, conversion, or revenue data. Its growth story is also weakly attributable because only two days of tracked history are available and no acquisition channel is identified.

If it were you

The opportunity is a faster, budget-first weekly planner with a tightly scoped community layer. Start with one language and one audience, verify plan completion and repeat usage, and define the paid moment before building a broad marketplace; the idea would be wrong if users generate a plan once but do not return, share, or pay.
