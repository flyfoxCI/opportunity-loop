#3 · Growth

Publish one before-and-after demo in the audience's exact community, show the completed result rather than a feature list, and attach a unique source link before publishing.

A visible transformation lowers interpretation cost and creates a traceable path from attention to activation, but the supplied cases do not establish that any demo or channel caused their measured revenue changes.

Who it suits, and when it doesn’t work

Suits solo builders with a repeatable core action that can be demonstrated quickly, access to a concentrated community, and the ability to add source links and basic product analytics. It is especially useful when prospects understand the problem but need a concrete example of the completed outcome.

Prerequisites

Requires one working demo, a destination page, unique source links, and separate tracking for source visits, signups, activation, and payment. The community must be selected from direct audience evidence rather than inferred from revenue, rankings, or broad discussion growth.

Risks

  1. Community rules may prohibit promotion
  2. Broad demos can attract curiosity without qualified users
  3. Attribution links can be stripped or ignored
  4. A completed result can expose implementation or quality weaknesses

Worth saying more

The cases support two typologies: a data workflow with a tangible before-and-after state and a short-form creation workflow whose output can appear across three platforms. Both make the result easier to understand, but neither investigation documents a demo, channel, cadence, or attributable acquisition source. Treat this play as an attribution discipline as much as a content tactic. The revenue increases and discussion counts are measured outcomes, not proof that community demos caused them; source-level records are required before repeating or scaling the tactic.
